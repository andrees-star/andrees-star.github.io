Client Segmentation

K-Means clustering (k=4) applied to FinCrece's full client base, identifying four behaviorally distinct segments across portfolio size, delinquency rate, product type and sector exposure. Optimal k selected via elbow method and silhouette analysis.

Segment 1 — Base Standard

Low portfolio, active clients

Clients with small individual balances and regular payment behavior. Predominantly Microcrédito Individual and Crédito Grupal Solidario in the Comercio and Alimentos sectors. Zero mora rate — lowest-risk segment.

⬤ Low Risk

Segment 2 — Low Risk

Mid portfolio, clean history

Mid-range portfolio clients with demonstrated repayment capacity. Mix of Crédito Rotativo (Manufactura, Agroindustria) and Línea Emprendimiento (Servicios). Positioned for credit line expansion.

⬤ Low Risk

Segment 3 — Mid Risk

Moderate mora, watch list

Clients showing early delinquency signals. Includes Línea Emprendimiento (Agroindustria) with mora rates near 7.9% and Microcrédito Individual (Servicios) approaching 31%. Requires proactive contact strategy.

⬤ Mid Risk

Segment 4 — High Risk

Critical mora, immediate action

Clients with mora rates reaching 100% (e.g. Crédito Rotativo — Agroindustria in Rionegro: full balance in default). Concentrated in specific product-sector combinations. Priority for provisioning and recovery campaigns.

⬤ High Risk

Geographic Optimization

PuLP (Linear Programming) applied to advisor routing across FinCrece's client locations, modeled as a variant of the Vehicle Routing Problem. Objective: minimize total travel distance while respecting daily visit capacity constraints per advisor.

🗺️ How the Optimization Works

Client coordinates were geocoded from their registered addresses across FinCrece's three operating cities. PuLP formulated a Mixed-Integer Linear Program: binary decision variables for each advisor-client assignment, with constraints on daily capacity, single-assignment (each client belongs to exactly one route), and return-to-base. The solver minimized haversine distance across all assigned routes. Optimized routes were rendered on interactive Folium maps, giving field supervisors a visual tool for planning daily visits.

📉 Impact

The unoptimized baseline — advisors visiting clients in sequential list order — generated unnecessary cross-city backtracking. Post-optimization routes cluster clients geographically, reducing total daily distance by 89.5%. For FinCrece's fleet of field advisors, this translates directly into fuel savings, more client visits per day, and reduced advisor fatigue — with no additional headcount required.

Project Methodology

A four-phase pipeline from raw microfinance data to actionable strategic output.

Phase 1 — Data Extraction & Cleaning

Raw client data extracted from FinCrece's core banking system. Pandas pipeline: duplicate removal, missing value imputation, currency normalization (COP), date standardization. Multi-index aggregation by city → product → sector for portfolio-level reporting.

Phase 2 — Client Segmentation (K-Means)

Feature engineering: portfolio balance per client, mora rate, credit product type (encoded), sector (encoded), days overdue. StandardScaler normalization. Elbow method + silhouette score converged on k=4. Segment profiles validated against FinCrece's credit risk taxonomy (Base Standard, Low Risk, Mid Risk, High Risk).

Phase 3 — Geographic Optimization (PuLP)

Client geocoding via address → coordinates lookup. PuLP Mixed-Integer Linear Program built per city. Decision variables: binary assignment of client j to advisor i. Constraints: capacity (max visits/day), coverage (all clients assigned), return-to-base. Solver: CBC (COIN-OR). Output routes rendered with Folium.

Phase 4 — Risk Scenario Analysis & Power BI Dashboard

Two macro scenarios modeled: Base Case (stable) and Stress (economic contraction, +15% mora rates). Key metrics computed per segment under each scenario: expected provision, coverage ratio, adjusted portfolio yield. Full 5-page interactive Power BI dashboard built: portfolio overview, segment deep-dive, geographic heatmap, scenario comparison, executive summary.
