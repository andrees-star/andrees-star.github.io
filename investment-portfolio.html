📉 Key Observations

The gap between ex-ante (+17.89%) and realized (−7.61%) performance highlights the inherent challenge of Capital Market Expectations estimation. The out-of-sample period captured a market regime with elevated interest rate volatility and sector rotation, particularly affecting the equity growth components (AVGO, NVDA, AAPL) that drove the optimization's high ex-ante forecast. Fixed-income holdings (TLT, IEF) also experienced mark-to-market losses during the rising-rate environment.

🔬 Methodology Limitation

Markowitz optimization is highly sensitive to return estimates. Small errors in expected return inputs can produce dramatically different realized outcomes — a well-documented phenomenon in the literature (Michaud, 1989). This project explicitly demonstrates this limitation as a learning objective, making the out-of-sample analysis the most pedagogically valuable section of the work.

Project Methodology

A structured, five-phase approach from universe definition to performance attribution.

Phase 1 — Asset Universe Definition

Selected 15 assets across three classes (equities, fixed income, alternatives) balancing sector diversification, liquidity and data availability. Asset selection criteria included market capitalization, correlation properties and sector coverage.

Phase 2 — Capital Market Expectations (CME)

Forward-looking return estimates built using an analyst-consensus and factor-based approach. Covariance matrix estimated from historical data with shrinkage to reduce estimation error. Risk-free rate calibrated to prevailing short-term Treasury yields.

Phase 3 — Mean-Variance Optimization

Markowitz (1952) framework implemented in Python (scipy.optimize). Efficient frontier mapped across the risk-return space. Optimal portfolio selected as the tangency portfolio (maximum Sharpe ratio). Constraints: long-only, full investment, per-asset weight caps.

Phase 4 — Investment Policy Statement

Formal IPS documentation defining investor objectives, risk tolerance, liquidity needs, time horizon and constraints — following CFA Institute standards. The IPS guided constraint calibration in the optimization step.

Phase 5 — Out-of-Sample Backtesting

Portfolio weights applied to a hold-out period with realized market data. Performance attribution disaggregated by asset class and individual security. Sharpe, Sortino, maximum drawdown and annualized return computed and compared to the ex-ante forecasts.

Key Conclusions

What this project demonstrates and teaches about modern portfolio management.

⚠️

Estimation Risk is Real

The 25.5 percentage-point return gap between ex-ante and realized demonstrates that CME accuracy is the binding constraint in Markowitz optimization — not the math itself.

🛡️

Diversification Provides Partial Protection

The −7.04% max drawdown, while negative, was contained relative to individual equity drawdowns during the same period, confirming the diversification benefit of multi-asset portfolios.

📐

Optimization Framework is Sound

Given accurate inputs, Markowitz MVO produces well-constructed, efficient portfolios. The framework's sensitivity to inputs motivates robust estimation techniques (Black-Litterman, resampling).

📚

IPS Disciplines the Process

Formalizing investor constraints in an Investment Policy Statement before optimization prevents unconstrained solutions that are theoretically optimal but practically unimplementable.

🔄

Regime Sensitivity

The out-of-sample period captured a rate-hike cycle that invalidated the expected correlation structure between equities and bonds — a reminder that historical correlations are non-stationary.

🚀

Extensions Identified

Future work: Black-Litterman model to blend views with priors, resampled efficiency (Michaud), dynamic rebalancing triggers, and factor decomposition of active return vs. benchmark.
